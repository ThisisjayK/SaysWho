Citation auditing for AI answers

Does the cited page actually say that?

Every AI answer now comes with links attached. They are what makes it feel checked. They are also the part nobody clicks. SaysWho clicks them, reads what is there, and marks the answer sentence by sentence.

The question it asks

Not whether a claim is true. Whether its source says so.

A sentence can be perfectly true and hung on a page that never mentions it.

It can also be wrong and cited to a page that says exactly that, word for word. Only the first of those is a citation problem, and that is the only thing this checks. Fact checking is a different job and a much harder one.

Supported

A passage was quoted from the cited page, and a script confirmed that passage is really in the document that got fetched.

Partly supported

The page backs part of it, or a weaker version. Whatever the source attaches that the sentence leaves off is listed with the quote.

Not supported

The page was read and does not state this. That is a fact about the citation. It says nothing about the world.

Could not verify

No verdict stands. Either the source could not be read, or the verdict was thrown out. This is not evidence in either direction and it enters no rate.

How it works

Four steps, and a script checking the third one.

1 · Capture

Reads the answer out of the page, hashes it, lists every cited URL. Citations it could not reach get counted, not quietly dropped.

2 · Split

Breaks the answer into individual claims, each carrying the citations attached to it. Interface furniture and non-factual lines are set aside and named.

3 · Fetch and judge

Fetches every cited page and asks, per claim, whether that page supports it. A verdict of supported has to come with a quoted span.

4 · Verify the quote

A script confirms the quoted span really appears in the fetched document. A fabricated quote voids the verdict, and how often that happens is published.

Step four exists because the judge is a language model and language models invent quotes. Catching that is cheap. Trusting it would not be.

The part that is different

It refuses.

Given nothing to check against, it gives you nothing back.

A claim whose only source is a dead link comes back marked could not verify, and it is scored nowhere. When half or more of an answer's cited claims produce no standing verdict, that answer gets no support rate at all. A percentage over the readable leftovers would really be a percentage over whichever pages happened to load that afternoon.

There is no confidence score anywhere in the tool. Unauditable claims are counted, named, and kept out of every published number.

Where it runs

In your browser, against your own answers.

A Chrome extension captures the answer and draws the result over the page. A local Python server does the fetching and the judging, because a content script cannot fetch arbitrary cross-origin URLs, and because the gates and the quote check belong in one implementation, not two that can drift apart.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. The server binds to 127.0.0.1 and will not start if its judge cannot answer a health check.

Install it Read the limitations first

SaysWho is research software. You run it by hand, for as long as an audit takes, and it is not something to leave running.
